## 🟦 Module 1: The Foundation (Existence & Religion)
*Goal: Address the baseline questions about why religion exists and if it's necessary.*

**1.1 What is a Religion?**
*   *Concept:* Differentiate between *Mazhab* (a set of rituals) and *Deen* (a complete code of life/worldview). 
*   *Interactive element:* A sliding scale showing "Rituals" on one end and "Complete Worldview" on the other.

**1.2 Can We Live Without Religion?**
*   *Concept:* Yes, biologically and socially, we can. But *philosophically*, can we? Discuss the "Existential Void." Humans are meaning-seeking creatures. Without a higher framework, life reduces to biological survival and subjective, fleeting pleasures.
*   *Analogy:* Living without religion is like living in a house without a foundation; it stands for a while, but collapses under the weight of life's tragedies.

**1.3 Introduction to Islam**
*   *Concept:* Islam is not a "new" religion invented 1400 years ago. It is the eternal, natural disposition (*Fitrah*) of humanity. It means "submission to the Creator," which naturally results in "peace."

## 🟦 Module 2: The Limits of Intellect & The Need for Prophets
*Goal: Explain why human philosophy isn't enough and why Prophethood is logically necessary.*

**2.1 The Triumph and Failure of Philosophers**
*   *Concept:* Human intellect (*Aql*) is a brilliant tool for understanding the *physical* world (science) and basic *moral* instincts. However, it fails at **Teleology** (the ultimate purpose of life).
*   *Examples:* Mention how great minds (Socrates, Kant, Nietzsche, Camus) reached dead ends. Nietzsche gave us the "Death of God" and the "Ubermensch," leading to nihilism. Existentialists say "create your own meaning," which is subjective and ultimately meaningless in the grand scheme. 
*   *The Verdict:* Intellect can tell us *how* the universe works, but not *why* it exists.

**2.2 Why Can't We Find the Purpose Without a Prophet?**
*   *Concept:* If a manufacturer creates a machine, only the manual from the manufacturer tells you its purpose. Humans are the creation; God is the Creator. 
*   *The 3 things Intellect cannot figure out on its own:*

    2.  The specific boundaries of Divine Law (what exactly pleases God).
    3.  The ultimate, objective purpose of human existence (to worship/serve God and act as His vicegerent).
*   *Conclusion:* Prophets are not just "magic miracle workers"; they are the ultimate teachers and the living manuals of human existence.

*   *Historical Context:* Explain that early divisions were largely **political** (who should rule after the Prophet), not theological. Over centuries, political factions morphed into theological sects.

**4.2 Which Firqah is Right?**

*   *Interactive Element:* A visual showing a pure stream of water (Original Islam) branching into muddy puddles (Sects), and an arrow pointing back to the pure stream.

**4.3 What About Those Never Introduced to Islam?**
*   *Concept:* This is crucial for showing God's perfect justice.

*   *The Unexposed (Ahl al-Fatrah / Mustad'afun):* If someone never received the message of Islam, or received a highly distorted, ugly version of it (e.g., through terrorism or extreme bias), they are not held accountable for rejecting it. 
*   *The Outcome:* On the Day of Judgment, they will be given a separate, specific test by God. God’s mercy and justice are absolute; He will not send a sincere, uninformed person to Hell simply because of geographical or historical circumstances.

### 🎨 Updated UI/UX Design Philosophy: Micro-Imagery

1. **Size & Placement:** Images should be small (e.g., 64x64dp to 120x120dp). They can be placed as:
   - Small circular icons next to module headings.
   - Subtle, softly rounded rectangular thumbnails breaking up long text blocks.
   - Faint, low-opacity (10-15%) background textures behind quotes or key takeaways.
2. **Style:** Abstract, minimalist, nature-inspired, or macro photography. Avoid literal images of people, mosques, or overtly religious clichés. The goal is to evoke *feeling* and *contemplation*.
3. **Technical Implementation (Android):** 
   - Use an image-loading library like **Coil** or **Glide** for efficient caching and smooth scrolling.
   - Apply subtle `rounded corners` (e.g., 12dp or 16dp) to all images to maintain the soft, clean aesthetic.
   - Use a slight `ColorFilter` or overlay if the image is near text, ensuring perfect readability.

### 💡 Pro-Tip for App Development (Android)

To ensure these small Unsplash images load perfectly and maintain the "clean view":

1. **Use a Consistent Aspect Ratio:** Force all small images into a `1:1` (square) or `4:3` aspect ratio with rounded corners (`ShapeAppearanceOverlay` in Material Design). This creates visual rhythm.
2. **Lazy Loading:** Since you are pulling from the web, use **Coil** (recommended for Kotlin) to handle image fetching. It’s lightweight and prevents the app from stuttering.
   ```kotlin
   // Example Coil implementation for a small image
   ImageView(context).load("https://images.unsplash.com/photo-...") {
       crossfade(true)
       placeholder(R.drawable.placeholder_subtle_pattern)
       transformations(RoundedCornersTransformation(16f))
   }
   ```
3. **Fallback Design:** If an image fails to load (due to no internet), have a beautiful, solid-color placeholder with a subtle Islamic geometric pattern or a simple vector icon so the UI never looks "broken."
